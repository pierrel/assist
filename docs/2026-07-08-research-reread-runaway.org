#+DATE: <2026-07-08>
#+STATUS: DESIGN TO READ (no code). Non-blocking. Guidance-first per AGENTS.md §"prefer guidance over middleware".

* Provenance note (why this doc was rewritten)

The first draft of this doc diagnosed a *cross-dispatch re-dispatch* runaway (the
orchestrator re-dispatching its searcher, per-run guards resetting each dispatch).
Live forensics on thread =20260708090812-b37c712b= (a "where is he buried?" turn)
disproved that. The actual mechanism is simpler and different, and the fix is the
*opposite* of "add a new bound": we deliberately *removed* a guard we already have
from the one agent that needed it. This rewrite is grounded entirely in that thread's
logs — measure, don't argue.

* TL;DR

Under *search-unavailable*, the research *orchestrator* (=create_research_agent=,
which has its own =read_url= tool) **fabricates plausible-looking URLs, gets 404, and
loops** — "Let me try completely different sources now" → guess another URL → 404 →
repeat, for ~90 min. Forensics on b37c712b: **108,278 "404 Not Found"** results vs
**10** successes; **0** successful searches (search was unavailable, 139 hits); the
model cycled fabricated URLs on a handful of real domains (=essence.com=,
=billboard.com=, =hotnewhiphop=, =complex=, =ajc=) — pages that do not exist.

*Why nothing stopped it:* the guard that exists precisely to block fabricated URLs —
=UrlProvenanceMiddleware= — is **NOT on the orchestrator**. It is wired only on the
*searcher* and *fact-checker* sub-agents (=agent.py:617,636=), deliberately excluded
from the orchestrator (=agent.py:650-654=) out of a fear of "re-dispatch thrash." And
the generic =LoopDetectionMiddleware= *is* on the orchestrator (=agent.py:496=) but
structurally can't catch this (see §2). So the orchestrator's own =read_url= is
un-provenanced, un-reread-bounded, and only nominally loop-detected — free to guess
404s until =recursion_limit= (which is far too loose to matter).

*The honest "are we causing our own bug?" answer:* **yes — by omission.** This is not
"too much middleware." It's the inverse: we **removed** a load-bearing guard
(provenance) from the orchestrator based on a hypothetical (re-dispatch thrash) that is
*less bad* than the unbounded 404 loop we actually got.

*Recommended fix (minimal, no new middleware):*
1. *(primary)* Give the orchestrator the **same guard pair the searcher/fact-checker
   already have** — =[UrlProvenanceMiddleware(), ReadUrlRereadBreaker()]= — making the
   three =read_url=-bearing agents symmetric. Provenance blocks the fabricated-URL
   404 loop by construction; the reread breaker bounds re-reads of *real* URLs (the
   older peptides shape). This *reuses* existing guards where they were wrongly
   omitted — it adds no new middleware class.
2. *(guidance, to neutralize the original exclusion rationale)* When search is
   unavailable / returns nothing, the orchestrator must **stop and report "couldn't
   look this up," not fabricate URLs and not re-dispatch.** This is what makes
   re-adding provenance safe: the exclusion feared "rejected fetch → re-dispatch →
   more searches"; the guidance says the correct response to no-sources is to *finish*,
   not to thrash. Same root as the queued =research-rate-limit-resilience= item.
3. *(message copy)* Provenance's rejection text must not say "read one of the URLs your
   search returned" when there are none — it should say "you have no sources; report
   that you couldn't find this and stop."

Do NOT build a new cross-dispatch bound (the first draft's idea) — it targets a
mechanism this runaway doesn't use.

* 1. Forensic timeline (thread 20260708090812-b37c712b, grounded in the logs)

Measured: 108,278 "404 Client Error: Not Found" read_url results, 10 successes, 0
successful searches, 139 "search unavailable" hits, ~800+ orchestrator messages, ~90
min on the single llama.cpp slot.

- *T0* — General agent dispatches the orchestrator **once** for "where is he buried?"
  (Pierre confirmed from the UI: one dispatch of the research subagent — this is *not*
  a re-dispatch loop).
- *T1* — Orchestrator dispatches the *searcher* (which has search_internet). **Search
  is UNAVAILABLE** (SearXNG rate-limited/down): =_SEARCH_UNAVAILABLE_MESSAGE=, no
  result URLs. The searcher returns nothing usable.
- *T2* — Orchestrator now has **no real URLs** and a question to answer. It has
  =read_url= and no provenance guard. It **fabricates** a plausible URL — e.g.
  =https://www.essence.com/rich-homie-quan-homegoing-celebration-of-life/= — a
  URL-shaped *guess*, not a search result.
- *T3* — =read_url= on the fabricated URL → **404 Not Found** (the page doesn't exist).
- *T4* — Model emits *"Let me try completely different sources now."* and fabricates
  another (=billboard.com/.../rich-homie-quan-funeral-...=, =hotnewhiphop=, =complex=,
  =ajc=) → 404. Repeat T2→T4 indefinitely.
- *Tn* — 108k 404s later, still looping. =recursion_limit=500= is the only ceiling and
  it's nowhere near tripped in a way that helps (each "step" is cheap; the loop just
  runs). No guard fires (§2). ~90 min gone.

Contrast with the older *peptides* shape (61 reads / 9 URLs, springer 22×): there the
orchestrator re-read a handful of *real* URLs (from a prior report / the one search)
rather than fabricating 404s. **Both are the same root gap** — the orchestrator's own
=read_url= is unguarded — differing only in whether the URLs are fabricated (b37c712b:
provenance would catch) or real-but-re-read (peptides: the reread breaker would catch).
The guard *pair* covers both.

* 2. Middleware audit — is a guard CAUSING it, or MISSING?

** =UrlProvenanceMiddleware= — MISSING from the orchestrator; this is the root cause
Provenance refuses any =read_url= whose URL was not produced by a prior search/tool
result (=url_provenance.py=), which is *exactly* the fabricated-URL case. It is wired on
the searcher (=:617=) and fact-checker (=:636=) but **not** the orchestrator (=:650-654=).
The exclusion comment: guarding the orchestrator's own read_url "risked re-dispatch
thrash (a rejected fetch → re-dispatch research → more searches); the orchestrator's
runaway is bounded by recursion_limit instead." Forensics show that trade was wrong:
the un-provenanced orchestrator produced an *unbounded* 404 loop, far worse than the
bounded re-dispatch it feared. **Verdict: add provenance to the orchestrator.** Neutralize
the original fear with guidance (#2 below), not by leaving the guard off.

** =ReadUrlRereadBreaker= — also MISSING from the orchestrator
Caps N reads of the *same* URL per run, finalize-not-kill. On searcher/fact-checker
(=:617,636=), not the orchestrator. It wouldn't have stopped b37c712b (fabricated URLs
differ each time → provenance is the one that catches that), but it *is* what bounds the
peptides-style re-read-real-URLs shape. **Verdict: add it too, as the orchestrator's guard
pair — symmetric with the sub-agents.** Reuse, not new.

** =LoopDetectionMiddleware= — PRESENT on the orchestrator, structurally blind to this
On the orchestrator (=:496=). Two patterns (=loop_detection.py=): (A) same *mutating*
tool + same error, consecutive; (B) same tool + same *args*, consecutive. b37c712b slips
both: =read_url= is **read-only** (transparent to Pattern A's mutating-tool walk), so
108k read_url *errors* don't count; and the model **varies the fabricated URL** each
call, so Pattern B never sees identical args ≥ threshold in a row. This gap is
*deliberate* (distinct-arg exploration is legitimate; per PR #128 the detector was
rolled back to 2 exact-repeat patterns to avoid misfires). *Verdict:* do NOT widen
LoopDetection to "cap tool errors" or "cap read-only repeats" — that's exactly the
ambiguous-signal heuristic the project rejects (legit multi-source research also errors
on some URLs). Provenance is the unambiguous, by-construction guard here.

** =SearchUnavailableBreakerMiddleware= — NEUTRAL; the trigger, not the loop
Search-unavailable is the *trigger* (no real URLs → the orchestrator improvises). The
breaker fires on the *searcher*, not the orchestrator. Its role in the fix is guidance
(#2): the orchestrator must treat "search unavailable / no sources" as a *stop*, not a
prompt to fabricate.

** =ToolResultToFileMiddleware=, =BadRequestRetry=, =EmptyResponseRecovery=, others — NEUTRAL / amplifiers
None cause the loop. Retry middlewares amplify *wall-clock* once the context is huge
(re-sending it), but they don't drive the fabrication. No change.

*Audit bottom line:* No middleware is *causing* the loop by over-firing. The cause is a
**missing** guard (provenance) on the orchestrator — a guard we already run on the peer
agents. The fix removes an inconsistency; it does not add a layer. That is the precise
answer to "I don't want to paper over an issue we're causing": we caused it by omission,
and the fix is to stop omitting.

* 3. Root-cause fix (minimal; reuse existing guards + guidance)

** (a) Give the orchestrator the sub-agents' guard pair (PRIMARY, by construction)
=agent.py:650-654= — change the orchestrator's middleware to include
=UrlProvenanceMiddleware()= and =ReadUrlRereadBreaker()=, matching =:617= / =:636=. This
makes the fabricated-URL 404 loop *unreachable* (provenance rejects a URL no search
produced) and bounds real-URL re-reads. No new middleware class; delete the special-case
exclusion. Also update the exclusion paragraph in =url_provenance.py='s module docstring
(=:23-28=), which currently documents "NOT the orchestrator … bounded by recursion_limit."

*Why provenance is transparent on the healthy path (resolves the original fear + open
Q1).* =_seen_urls= (=url_provenance.py:95-117=) allows any URL present in *any*
=ToolMessage= (or the user message) in the agent's context. The searcher returns its
findings to the orchestrator **as a =task= tool result — a =ToolMessage=** — so every
URL the searcher actually found lands in the orchestrator's context and provenance lets
the orchestrator read it. Prior-report URLs the orchestrator loads via =read_file= are
likewise in a =ToolMessage=. So on the healthy path the guard is invisible; it only bites
a URL that appears in *no* tool result — i.e. a pure fabrication, which under
search-unavailable (b37c712b) is *every* URL the orchestrator tried. (Caveat to verify in
the eval: this relies on the searcher's returned findings text actually *containing* the
URLs — research citations normally do; the healthy-path regression eval is the check.)

** (b) Guidance: no sources → stop, don't fabricate, don't re-dispatch (makes (a) safe)
=research_instructions.txt.j2= + =sub_research.txt.j2=: when search is unavailable or
returns nothing, the correct action is to **report "I couldn't look this up" and finish**
— explicitly *not* to guess URLs and *not* to re-dispatch the searcher. This neutralizes
the exact "re-dispatch thrash" the original exclusion feared, so provenance can go on the
orchestrator without the downside that motivated leaving it off. Prefer an argument-shaped
rule (AGENTS.md §"instruction's shape"): "Only call =read_url= on a URL that appeared in a
search result or a file you read. If you have none, stop and say so."

** (c) Provenance rejection copy (small) — and it must fit the ORCHESTRATOR, which has no search_internet
=_correction= (=url_provenance.py:132-141=) currently ends "…run search_internet to find
the page" and, when the allow-list is empty, "(none yet — run search_internet first)".
That advice is correct for the *searcher* (it has =search_internet=) but *wrong for the
orchestrator*, whose tools are =[read_url]= only — it cannot run search. When the
allow-list is empty (no sources in context) the message should tell *any* agent to
**stop and report it couldn't find sources**, not to run a tool the orchestrator lacks.
Keep the "read one of these" branch for when URLs *are* listed. Small copy change, no new
logic beyond keying on "allow-list empty".

** (d) recursion_limit — leave it
Once (a) bounds the loop by construction, =recursion_limit=500= is a harmless backstop.
Don't tune it here.

* 4. Risks & open questions for Pierre

1. *(RESOLVED — verified against =url_provenance.py=.)* Does provenance on the
   orchestrator hurt legitimate deep research? No: =_seen_urls= allows any URL in a
   =ToolMessage=, and the searcher's findings reach the orchestrator *as* a =task=
   =ToolMessage=, so search-sourced URLs pass; prior-report URLs (via =read_file=) pass.
   The one residual: the searcher's findings text must *contain* the URLs (citations
   normally do) — the healthy-path regression eval is the gate. Residual open Q: if the
   orchestrator ever legitimately builds a URL from a *pattern* (base + id) rather than
   copying a search result, provenance would block it — confirm from real transcripts;
   if it happens, that path should route through the searcher, not the orchestrator.
2. *Guidance vs the small model.* (b) tells the model to stop on no-sources; the model
   ignores stop-prose in general (why the breakers exist). Here we don't *rely* on it —
   provenance (a) enforces the hard part by construction; (b) only shapes the *finish*
   message. So (a) carries correctness even if (b) is soft.
3. *The peptides re-read shape* is covered by the reread breaker half of the pair, but
   confirm from the peptides logs that its reads were of *real* (search-sourced) URLs —
   if they were also fabricated, provenance alone suffices and the reread breaker is
   belt-and-suspenders.
4. *Blast radius:* this touches =agent.py= middleware wiring + prompts → full three-phase
   treatment. It's a *removal of an exclusion* (small diff) but on the research hot path.
5. *LoopDetection stays as-is* — we explicitly reject widening it to error/read-only
   heuristics (misfire risk). If Pierre wants a generic backstop anyway, that's a separate
   conversation with the guardrail-misfire trade-off on the table.
