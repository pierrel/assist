#+TITLE: Research re-read runaway — timeline, middleware-interference audit, root-cause fix
#+DATE: <2026-07-08>
#+STATUS: DESIGN TO READ (no code). Non-blocking. Guidance-first per AGENTS.md §"prefer guidance over middleware".

* TL;DR

The runaway is *not caused by an over-eager middleware*. It is caused by a
structural + guidance gap: the research *orchestrator* (=create_research_agent='s
main graph, prompted by =research_instructions.txt.j2=) *re-dispatches* its inner
searcher/fact-check sub-agent many times within one turn, and *every* one of our
=read_url=/search guards is *per-run (per-dispatch) scoped*, so each fresh dispatch
resets their counters to zero. Under search-unavailable the orchestrator keeps
re-dispatching because a thin/failed result reads to it as "the research genuinely
failed → try again," and nothing at the orchestrator level bounds that.

So this is *the same root cause* as the queued =research-rate-limit-resilience=
item: *search-unavailable → orchestrator re-dispatch → per-run guards dilute → 61
reads across 9 URLs over 60-96 min.* The honest "are we causing our own bug?"
answer: we are not *causing* it with a bad guard, but we *are* failing to bound it
at the one place the turn history actually persists (the orchestrator), and one
guard (=UrlProvenanceMiddleware=) *mildly worsens* it by steering fabricated-URL
attempts back onto the already-read URLs.

*Recommended fix, in priority order (all guidance-first):*
1. *(guidance, primary)* Close the re-dispatch loophole in
   =research_instructions.txt.j2= — a search-unavailable or thin result is *not*
   grounds to re-dispatch; relay + stop. Make the searcher reliably *surface* the
   unavailable status to the orchestrator even below the breaker threshold.
2. *(guidance, primary)* Fold in the partial-results branch (use what succeeded)
   so "some searches failed" stops discarding good results — the resilience item.
3. *(minimal guard, only if the eval shows guidance can't carry it)* A single
   cross-dispatch bound *at the orchestrator* (where history persists): cap
   =task(research-agent)= re-dispatches per turn, keyed on the unambiguous
   unavailable constant — finalize-not-kill. This is the "orchestrator re-dispatch
   bound" fork the reread-breaker doc explicitly deferred
   (=docs/2026-07-07-read-url-reread-breaker.org:88-91=).

Do *not* add a fourth =read_url= guard, and do *not* widen the per-run breakers —
they are structurally blind to re-dispatch (=subagents.py:528=), so more of them
just adds middleware without touching the multiplier.

* 1. Timeline of the runaway (concrete trace, grounded in code)

Reproduced on threads =20260707142207=, =20260708124314=; also "history of San
Francisco" / "where is he buried". Measured: ~61 =read_url= across ~9 distinct URLs
(springer 22x, usada 18x, fda 16x, pubmed 14x, wikipedia 10x); 300-400+ messages;
60-96 min holding the single llama.cpp slot.

- *T0* — User asks a research question. Trunk (general agent, =recursion_limit=500=,
  =thread.py:185=) dispatches the *orchestrator* via =task= (=agent.py:326-339=).
  Fresh orchestrator state = one =HumanMessage= (=subagents.py:528=).
- *T1* — Orchestrator runs Step 0 (=research_instructions.txt.j2:10-17=): =ls('/')=,
  maybe =read_file= a *prior report on the same topic*. First source of the
  "old/prior-report URLs" the runaway later re-reads: the prior report's citation
  URLs enter the orchestrator's context here.
- *T2* — Orchestrator dispatches the *searcher* once (=research_instructions.txt.j2:19=
  "Dispatch it ONCE"). Fresh searcher state = one =HumanMessage= (=subagents.py:528=).
- *T3* — Searcher calls =search_internet=. *Search is UNAVAILABLE* (SearXNG
  rate-limited/down) → =tools.py:57-61,206-278= returns the exact
  =_SEARCH_UNAVAILABLE_MESSAGE= (=tools.py:48-54=). No result URLs come back.
- *T4* — Searcher has *no new URLs*. Its prompt says STOP on unavailable
  (=sub_research.txt.j2:21=), but the small model is unreliable here (why the breakers
  exist). It tries =read_url=:
  - On a *fabricated* URL (a guessed "correct" PMID/product page) →
    =UrlProvenanceMiddleware= refuses (=url_provenance.py:156-188=) and returns a
    correction *listing the already-seen URLs* and saying *"Read one of the URLs your
    search returned"* (=url_provenance.py:132-141=). *This actively channels the model
    back onto the handful of known URLs* — the worsening interaction (see §2).
  - On an *already-seen* URL (prior-report citation, or one read once) → provenance
    passes; =ToolResultToFileMiddleware= offloads the page and hands back a ~600-char
    preview + "grep the file" (=tool_result_to_file.py:66-79=). The sought fact is not
    in the preview, so the model re-reads hoping for a different result.
- *T5* — =ReadUrlRereadBreaker= (max_reads=3, =read_url_reread_breaker.py:33,103=) caps
  *this run* at 3 fetches/URL and returns "answer from what you have; stop"
  (=:110-120=). =LoopDetectionMiddleware= Pattern B (=loop_detection.py:288-343=) would
  also stop *consecutive* identical calls at 3. So *within this one dispatch* reads are
  bounded (~3-5/URL). The searcher returns *thin findings*.
  - Note: =SearchUnavailableBreakerMiddleware= threshold is 4 unavailable searches
    *per run* (=agent.py:598=, =search_unavailable_breaker.py:59-74,123=). A run that
    stops after ~2 searches never reaches 4 → *breaker does not fire*, and the returned
    message may *not* contain the exact unavailable constant. So the orchestrator gets
    "thin findings," not a clean "search is down."
- *T6* — Orchestrator is *unsatisfied* with thin findings. The prompt loophole
  (=research_instructions.txt.j2:19=: "re-dispatch only if the first result genuinely
  failed to address the question at all") reads, to the model, as license: a
  search-starved thin result *looks* like a genuine failure → it *re-dispatches the
  searcher*.
- *T7* — *Fresh searcher state resets every per-run guard to zero* (=subagents.py:528=;
  reread breaker counts only from the last =HumanMessage=, =read_url_reread_breaker.py:9-11,48-70=).
  The searcher re-reads the *same* URLs up to 3 more times. Repeat T3→T6.
- *T8..Tn* — The multiplier. Incident forensics: ~16 orchestrator dispatches × ~5
  same-URL reads = 78x on one PubMed URL (=docs/2026-07-07-read-url-reread-breaker.org:54-72=).
  In the 2026-07 threads: 61 reads across 9 URLs. The fact-checker path does the same on
  a report with hallucinated citations: it can't *search* for correct ones, so it
  brute-forces re-reads of the cited URLs.
- *Context balloon* — Each dispatch appends its task-call =AIMessage= + returned
  =ToolMessage= to the *orchestrator's* history, plus the orchestrator's own
  =read_url=/=read_file=/synthesis. 300-400+ messages accumulate → each LLM call's
  prefill grows → each call slower. =BadRequestRetryMiddleware= (context-overflow 400s)
  and =EmptyResponseRecoveryMiddleware= retries re-send the huge context, amplifying
  wall-clock (§2). Result: 60-96 min on the single slot.
- *Terminal* — Eventually the orchestrator hits =recursion_limit=300= (=agent.py:672=),
  now *terminal* (=GraphRecursionError= excluded from =rollback_on=,
  =checkpoint_rollback.py:52,66-71=). The comment at =agent.py:671= claims "~5 min"; the
  incident ran far longer because per-call latency grows with the ballooned context and
  the re-dispatch multiplier is unbounded.

*Where each guard fires / doesn't:*

| Guard | Fires? | Why it does / doesn't bound the loop |
|-------+--------+--------------------------------------|
| =ReadUrlRereadBreaker= (max 3) | within each dispatch | per-run counter resets every dispatch (=subagents.py:528=); caps 3/URL/run, not /turn. Named residual. |
| =SearchUnavailableBreaker= (thr 4) | usually NOT | ~2 unavailable/run < 4 → diluted; inert on orchestrator (no =search_internet=). |
| =UrlProvenanceMiddleware= | on fabricated URLs | permits re-reads of known URLs; correction *lists* known URLs → mild steer back. |
| =LoopDetection= Pattern B | rarely | consecutive + same-args only; re-reads interspersed + cross-dispatch. |
| orchestrator =recursion_limit=300= | eventually (terminal) | the only aggregate bound — a blunt ceiling, not a semantic stop. |

* 2. Middleware-interference audit (Pierre's priority: are WE causing it?)

Verdicts are honest — most neutral; two mildly worsen; none is the root cause;
*none is a removal candidate*.

** =ReadUrlRereadBreaker= — NEUTRAL, correctly built, wrong scope for THIS bug
Does exactly what it says within a run (caps 3/URL, finalize-not-kill). *Not* causing
the loop. Limitation is scope: per-run counter resets each dispatch (=subagents.py:528=).
Documented residual (=.../2026-07-07-...org:54-72=), not a defect. *Not a removal
candidate.* Widening it is the last-resort §3(c) — and even then the *orchestrator*, not
this sub-agent guard, is the right home.

** =UrlProvenanceMiddleware= — MILD WORSENING (flag), NOT removal
Refusing a fabricated URL is correct + load-bearing (prevents the 101 GB dead-URL flood,
=:1-11=). *But* under search-unavailable it has a perverse second-order effect: when the
model tries to *escape* the known-URL set by guessing a "correct" URL, provenance refuses
and its correction (=:132-141=) *hands back the list of already-seen URLs and says "Read
one of the URLs your search returned."* That pushes the model back into re-reading the
same handful — the runaway's inner loop. *Verdict:* keep the guard; *tune the correction
copy* so that when no new URLs exist it says "you have the sources you're going to get;
write from them or stop," not "read one of these again." A message change, not a new guard.

** =SearchUnavailableBreakerMiddleware= — NEUTRAL but INEFFECTIVE; trigger-side fix belongs here
Not causing the loop. But its per-run threshold (4, =:123=) is diluted by re-dispatch
(~2/run), so it *rarely fires* — and when it doesn't, the orchestrator never receives the
clean "search is down" signal (=:77-94=, worded to match =research_instructions.txt.j2:21=).
*This is the crux of the trigger side:* the stop-signal often never crosses the agent
boundary. *Verdict:* make unavailability *reliably surface* to the orchestrator (searcher
relays unavailable even below threshold when zero searches succeeded), rather than lowering
the threshold (which would misfire on healthy bursts).

** =LoopDetectionMiddleware= — NEUTRAL; structurally can't catch this
Two exact-repeat, consecutive-only patterns (=:288-343=). Re-reads are non-consecutive +
cross-dispatch. Gap is *deliberate* (distinct-arg exploration is legit). Not a cause.

** =BadRequestRetryMiddleware= + =EmptyResponseRecoveryMiddleware= — AMPLIFIERS of wall-clock, not causes
Neither re-dispatches or re-reads. But once the context balloons, a context-overflow
=BadRequestError= makes =BadRequestRetry= re-send the huge context up to 3x
(=bad_request_retry.py:196-243=), and =EmptyResponseRecovery= can retry once more
(=empty_response_recovery.py:242-268=). On a 300+-message context each is a slow prefill.
*Verdict:* they *amplify the 60-96 min*, they don't *cause* the loop. Correct defenses; no
change. Worth noting so a log reader doesn't mistake retry spam for the loop.

** =ToolResultToFileMiddleware= — MILD WORSENING (minor), NOT removal
Offloading the page + preview (=:66-79=) is essential context-bounding (the I/O exception
the ethos allows). *But* when the sought fact isn't in the preview, the small model re-reads
instead of =grep=-ing — a minor contributor to the *urge* to re-read. Re-reading returns the
identical preview (provably useless; the reread breaker catches it within a run). *Verdict:*
keep; prompt reinforcement (the reread-breaker message already does this).

** =WriteCollision=, =OutputSanitization=, =JsonValidation=, =ToolNameSanitization=, =ThreadQueue=, =GitPushBlocker=, =ModelLogging= — NEUTRAL
None touches =read_url=/=search_internet=/=task= in a way that could cause re-dispatch/re-read.

*Audit bottom line:* No middleware is the root cause, and *nothing should be removed.* Two
mild worseners (UrlProvenance correction copy; ToolResultToFile preview instinct) are
*message/guidance* tweaks, not structural. The real gap is (a) the orchestrator re-dispatch
loophole in the prompt and (b) unavailability not reliably surfacing across the sub-agent
boundary. Guidance problem first; only if guidance provably can't carry it, a *single
orchestrator-level* bound — not a fourth per-run guard.

* 3. Root-cause fix (guidance-first, minimal), in priority order

** (a) Is an interfering middleware the cause? — NO. Nothing to remove.
No guard is causing the loop. The only middleware-side edits are *message copy*
(provenance correction) — not a new layer. So we do not fix this by removing/adding
middleware first. Satisfies "don't paper over an issue WE'RE causing" + "middleware is
last resort".

** (b) GUIDANCE / prompt fix (PRIMARY) — coordinated edits, eval-gated
Same root cause as =research-rate-limit-resilience= (=roadmap.org:99-141=).
- *(b1) Orchestrator — close the re-dispatch loophole* (=research_instructions.txt.j2=):
  today line 19 permits re-dispatch "if the first result genuinely failed"; the loophole
  is that a search-starved *thin* result reads as a "genuine failure." Change to an
  argument-shaped rule (AGENTS.md §"instruction's shape"): "You may issue at most ONE
  =task(research-agent)= call for this question. If it returns unavailable or thin, do not
  call it again — finalize with what you have or relay the unavailable status and stop."
- *(b2) Searcher — reliably surface unavailability* (=sub_research.txt.j2= + reuse the
  =SearchUnavailableBreaker= composed message): when *zero* searches succeed, the searcher's
  *final message* carries the unavailable status verbatim (=search_unavailable_breaker.py:77-94=),
  so the orchestrator's stop-branch has an unambiguous string to match even below threshold.
- *(b3) Partial-results resilience* (=roadmap.org:123-131=): distinguish "backend fully
  down (0 results ever) → relay + stop" from "burst tripped engine limits, some results in
  hand → keep the good ones, research from them, report partial honestly." Prevents the
  *opposite* failure (giving up with a good result in hand) and removes a second reason the
  orchestrator thrashes.
- *(b4) Provenance correction copy* (§2): when no new URLs are available, stop inviting
  re-reads of exhausted URLs.

** (c) LAST RESORT — one cross-dispatch bound AT THE ORCHESTRATOR (only if the eval shows guidance can't carry it)
If, after (b), the eval still shows re-dispatch under search-unavailable, add *one* bound —
at the *orchestrator*, not a fourth per-run sub-agent guard. Rationale: the orchestrator's
own turn history *persists across its dispatches* (each =task= call + returned =ToolMessage=
accumulates in the orchestrator graph), so a count there *is* cross-dispatch by construction.
*Shape (coarse, unambiguous, finalize-not-kill):* an =after_model=/=wrap_tool_call= middleware
on the orchestrator counting completed =task(research-agent)= dispatches this turn (reuse
=loop_detection._extract_events=). Trigger, cheapest first:
1. *Unavailable-keyed (preferred):* if the most recent =task(research-agent)= result contains
   the unavailable constant, refuse a further dispatch + inject the honest "couldn't look
   this up" finalize. Keys on an exact constant (coarse real bound, not a fuzzy heuristic).
   Requires (b2).
2. *Count-keyed (backstop):* cap =task(research-agent)= at N/turn (e.g. 2-3); on the (N+1)th,
   finalize with what's in hand. Blunter; only if (1)'s signal proves unreliable.
*Why not thread reread-state across dispatches?* You'd inject shared state into each fresh
sub-agent invocation (fighting =subagents.py:528=; sub-agents use their own =InMemorySaver=).
The orchestrator-level count is strictly simpler and lives where the data already is. This is
the "orchestrator re-dispatch bound" the reread doc deferred (=.../2026-07-07-...org:88-91=).
